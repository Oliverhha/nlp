gregg araki's the doom generation is reminiscent of everything from my own private idaho to the river's edge to true romance to the experimental films of pasolini , of warhol ( morrisey ) , as well as of richard kern . the film reveals its thematic message when the most innocent and selfless of its three main characters asks the other , more self - centered , two if they ever think about the meaning of existence . dismissing the very question , they reveal to the questioner an answer of sorts , one which suggests that we each create a meaning for ourselves , and are all existentially alone as we do so . while offering us a rather slight story of a pair of teen lovers on the road who encounter a slightly older bisexual who becomes their nemesis , companion , lover and protector , the doom generation offers a great deal of visual style and wit , and some genuine moments of suspense . in fact , the film's gory and discomforting climactic scene is perhaps the artistic highlight and suggests some real filmaking talent by writer / director gregg araki . this is probably not everyone's cup of tea , but is worth a look for those who like a film which challenges them to react to strong imagery and who don't mind transgressive depictions .
