lord knows rod serling tried to write comedy for his great series , but the results were almost always trite and heavy - handed . this one , though often maligned by the " experts " , is truly delightful ( and yes . . . i realize that comedy is very subjective ) . there are some slow , repetitious moments in the main confrontation between rance and the real jesse ( which could have been solved with more inventive direction ) . . . . but otherwise , chris nyby's work is first - rate . the entire first half is just crazy - - a beautifully staged " chamber - comedy " in which we find ourselves enmeshed in the quirky interplay between a tedious , pampered boor and the behind - the - scenes mechanics of tv make - believe . and for once , here's a running gag that actually works ! - - - rance's bad habit of wildly flinging his six - shooter in the direction of the mirror mounted behind the bar . the real pitfall of this brand of satire is the tendency to play too broadly . . but larry blyden's performance is beautifully balanced and controlled , as is that of robert cornthwaite , who could always be counted on for a first - rate job . arch johnson is also dead - on . from rance's nutty first entrance to the hilarious concluding plot twist , serling managed to crank out his best comedy while buried under the stress of his third full season of production . and fred steiner's ( uncredited ) " hoe - down " music adds the final touch of brilliance to this underrated gem .
