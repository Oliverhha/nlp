i saw this film on september 22 at the atlantic film festival . i was with some people who had never heard anything heavier than kiss and wouldn't know black sabbath from the black label society . it's a mark of this film's excellence that they really enjoyed it and felt they had gained an understanding of the metal scene . this is no gushing fan - tribute . well , it is in part , but i couldn't stay objective either were i given the chance to sit down with bruce dickinson at the hammersmith odeon . seriously , it rises far above the fulfillment of a metal fan's dreams to explore many different elements of what makes metal both an enduring artform and one that remains on the fringes . the structure of the film follows distinct topics - gender , censorship , death & violence , and more . each area is thoughtfully explored with comments from many musicians and some outside observers . from the calm commentary of rob zombie to the wry humour of dio to the laughable minimalist interviews with norwegian black - metal players , there's a broad range of experiences and opinions . this is an excellent documentary in every way . i knew it was going to be great when i got my first glimpse of the big chart of metal bands that correctly put kiss far away from the actual musicians . ronnie james dio further obliged with a running commentary on gene simmon's preference for money over music . you'll get to see and hear a lot of great bands , from the standard warhorses like maiden , metallica , motorhead and slayer to the less - mainstream stuff like voivod .
