i have been looking forward to seeing the film version of gil courtemanche's book ' un dimanche à la piscine de kigali ' ; it was a disturbing yet beautiful film . the film takes , it seems at first , its sweet time in shaping itself up ; but it is merely a way to draw you into the story , making you a part of it . robert favreau did an incredible and sensitive job in being suggestive instead of using candid images but there are moments in the film that remain very hard to witness . by the time you are at the end of the film and the lights turn back on , you find yourself a bit stoned by the amount of emotions you have experienced . you then wish the film had taken even longer in presenting its different characters , or perhaps you wish you had paid better attention . you want to hate the film because it displays such a level of horror ; the hellish reality of the rwandan genocide is clear in this film . but ' kigali ' doesn't allow you to hate it as you see its love story take center stage and shining despite the darkness of the subject matter . . .
