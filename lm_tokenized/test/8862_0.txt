foyle's war has a good and quite believable wartime atmosphere , largely due to the good sets and costumes . the stories are also often good . there are some quite glaring problems , though . one is the anachronistic speech , for which the revered michael kitchen is often to blame . he tends towards the dreaded ( and very modern ) " uptalk " which turns normal statements into seeming questions , as in , " my name's foyle ? i'm a policeman ? " his extremely mannered performance is a bit of a trial overall , in my opinion . he manages to make underacting into a kind of overacting . some of the phraseology is too modern , too . for example , british people did not talk about the ' train station ' until very recent times . it was just called the station , or , in full , the railway station . foyle's two sidekicks are better than him . in particular , honeysuckle weeks excellently picks up on deborah kerr's role as colonel blimp's driver johnny ( in the famous 1943 film ) . she seems exactly in period and lifts the series .
