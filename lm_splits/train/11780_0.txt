"cannon" was probably the least pompous of the original entries in television's anti- establishment police/private-eye subgenre; setting the stage for "harry-o" and "the rockford files". rotund actor william conrad played the title character; dubbed "cannonball" in mad magazine's satire of the series. after years as the voice-over narrator of "the fugitive" and "rocky and bullwinkle", conrad finally was allowed to be on-screen and is said to have incorporated a lot of his own quirky characteristics into the frank cannon character. frank is a detective who only takes a case when he feels like it or his pressed for cash to maintain his expensive epicurean lifestyle (i.e. adventures in eating). most of the action in the series is in the form of car chases; there is some fighting but almost no running. lacking a handsome co-star "cannon's" producers were obviously ceding most potential female viewers to the other networks during this time slot; which is generally a good thing for those in the target demographic as there are no compromises to expand the audience.
