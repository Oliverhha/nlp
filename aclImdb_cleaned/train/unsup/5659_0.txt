the bad old days indeed! as a new yorker, i found this film to be a grim reminder of how much more lawless and dilapidated new york was during this time period. i won't rehash the generally accurate comments of the other reviewers other than to say that this is one of the best cinematic deglamorizations of crime (and law enforcement) ever. anthony franciosa is just great as a mafia enforcer. he's still the same nice, personable guy people my age remember from 60s and 70s television. you really want to be his friend. a great smile and a comforting manner. a demeanor he maintains without a crease while he maims, tortures and kills! one of the most skilled---and frightening---performances of all time. anthony quinn is also excellent as a corrupt police captain who has deluded himself into believing that he still has some ethics. the graphic violence, sex, profanity, and ethnic slurs take it light years out of the family viewing category and i wouldn't recommend it to anyone with a weak stomach. i don't think the "blaxploitation" label many apply to this film is accurate. the best i can think of is "noir verite". don't miss this one!
